## Pattern Summary: Common Structures in Shipboard-Robbery Scam

| Element | Pattern |
|---------|---------|
| **Spatial Enclosure** | Ship cabins, docks, and winding alleys are physically enclosed or visually blocked, making it hard for victims to seek help or give chase |
| **Identity Verification Gap** | Private boat hires and temporary porter hires lack identity registration or contractual binding; the true identities of boatmen and porters are only revealed after the transaction |
| **Power Asymmetry** | Travelers (especially those carrying valuables) are physically disadvantaged against boatman groups or local porters familiar with the terrain |
| **Time Pressure** | Appointment deadlines and travel schedules force victims to abandon pursuit (Magistrate Ni, Magistrate Jia); scammers exploit the time window to escape |
| **Signal Misreading** | Signals like luggage weight, wealthy appearance, and official status are read by scammers as "has money to rob"; victims don't realize they're broadcasting danger signals |
| **Insider Advantage** | Servants (Man Qi, Zhan Xing) and porters (Huang San) as "insiders" possess information advantages—can be leveraged (Man Qi saving his master) or deceived (Zhan Xing taking the wrong load) |
| **Modern Equivalent** | Ride-hailing crimes, hitchhiker hijacking, train-station illegal taxis, luggage托运 switch scams, tourist traps |

## Appendix: Ming Dynasty Slang Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **棍 / 光棍** | Swindler, rogue (Ming street slang) |
| **伙** | Accomplice, partner in crime |
| **牙** | Broker, middleman |
| **衙役** | Government runner, bailiff |
| **棍徒** | Gang member, thug |
| **作契对银** | Formal contract and silver exchange (standard Ming transaction) |
| **打抽丰 / 秋风** | Seasonal grafting — exploiting official connections for money/gifts |
| **龟奴** | Brothel pimp, procurer |
