## Pattern Summary: Common Structures in Strongarm Robbery

| Element | Pattern |
|---------|---------|
| **Timing Precision** | Choose the victim's most vulnerable moments: using the toilet, walking alone at night, exposing goods, focused on melting silver |
| **Sensory Deprivation** | Eye patches with plaster, lime thrown in eyes, violent surprise attacks—deprive vision or mobility to eliminate resistance |
| **Legitimacy Camouflage** | Frame the victim as a thief, forge marks, pretend to be a helper—drape violent robbery in a mantle of legitimacy |
| **Information Fabrication** | Private seals, dropped fake bags, "recovering stolen goods" narratives—create "evidence" favorable to the robber |
| **Low-Confrontation Design** | Avoid frontal conflict; use pain, chaos, and decoys to buy escape time |
| **Public-Power Arbitrage** | Exploit lazy judicial procedures (determining ownership by marks) or social prejudice (not helping "thieves") |
| **Modern Equivalent** | Snatch-and-run motorcycle theft, street knockout drugs, fake police body searches,碰瓷 followed by chaos theft |

## Appendix: Ming Dynasty Slang Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **棍 / 光棍** | Swindler, rogue (Ming street slang) |
| **伙** | Accomplice, partner in crime |
| **牙** | Broker, middleman |
| **衙役** | Government runner, bailiff |
| **棍徒** | Gang member, thug |
| **作契对银** | Formal contract and silver exchange (standard Ming transaction) |
| **打抽丰 / 秋风** | Seasonal grafting — exploiting official connections for money/gifts |
| **龟奴** | Brothel pimp, procurer |
